Formal objection

Mathematics says no.

A proof by induction appears to establish that every horse is the same horse. It is elegant, concise, and—regrettably—broken.

Theorem MHT-ΔALL HORSES ARE THE SAME HORSE
01

A group containing one horse contains only the same horse. Trivially true.

🐎
02

Assume any group of n horses is actually one horse. Consider n + 1.

🐎 🐎 🐎 🐎
03

Remove the last. The remaining n are “the same.” Remove the first. The other n are also “the same.”

🐎 🐎 🐎 🐎
🐎 🐎 🐎 🐎
04

Therefore all horses are the same horse.

Q.E.D.
ERROR FOUND

The n = 1 → n = 2 transition has no overlap.

🐎Group A
∩ = 0 horses
🐎Group B

The two one-horse subsets share no horse, so nothing connects their identities. The induction step fails exactly where the second horse arrives.

Actual math: This is a classic false-induction puzzle often phrased as “all horses are the same color.” The flaw occurs at the 1-to-2 case.
